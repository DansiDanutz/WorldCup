# WorldCup26.world YouTube Live Script

Target runtime: 18 to 22 minutes.

Format: live screen-share demo.

Main objective: get viewers to sign up, pick 3 teams, and begin collecting cards.

Tone: energetic, clear, football-first, not too technical.

## 0:00-1:00 - Opening Hook

Welcome everyone. Today I want to show you WorldCup26.world, and I want to explain why this is not just another football website.

This is a football prediction game, but it is also a collection app. It is built around the idea that football is not only matches and scores. Football is stories. It is nations, legends, impossible moments, heartbreak, heroes, underdogs, and memories that people keep for years.

So the idea is simple. You come into the app, you sign up, you pick 3 teams, and then you start following the World Cup through a different kind of experience. You are not only checking scores. You are collecting stories.

Every video we make on the WorldCup26 Legends YouTube channel can connect back into the app. You watch the story, you understand the moment, and then the card becomes something you can unlock and keep in your collection.

If you love football, if you love history, if you love cards, or if you like collecting rare digital artifacts, this app is made for you.

The link is WorldCup26.world. I will show you exactly how it works.

## 1:00-2:30 - Show The Homepage

On screen, show: `Promote/screenshots/01-home-desktop.png` or the live homepage.

This is the first thing people see when they arrive. The app is built to be simple. You do not need to understand a complicated fantasy system. You do not need to spend money. You do not need to manage a team every day.

The first action is very easy: pick 3 teams.

That is the entry point. You choose 3 nations that you believe in, or 3 nations you love, or 3 nations you want to follow through the tournament. Once you have your teams, you can watch how they perform and how your points move on the leaderboard.

The important part is that the app is free to start. The leaderboard is for football fun, community, and bragging rights.

So if someone is watching this live and asking, "What do I do first?" the answer is:

Go to WorldCup26.world. Sign up. Pick your 3 teams.

That is the first step into the whole experience.

## 2:30-4:00 - Explain The Prediction Structure

On screen, show the app dashboard or match schedule screenshot.

The prediction part gives people a reason to care every day. During the World Cup, there are matches all the time. Some are huge. Some are strange. Some involve nations people do not usually follow closely.

The app gives you a structure. You pick 3 teams and follow them.

There is also one very important fairness rule: if a user signs up late, they do not get points for old matches that already finished. Their points start from their sign-up and pick date.

That matters because it keeps the leaderboard clean. Someone cannot arrive after Germany already won a big match, pick Germany, and receive old points. You join, you pick, and from that point forward your score begins.

That makes the game easy to understand and fair for new users.

But the leaderboard is only one side of the app. The bigger side, and the part we are focusing on now, is the card collection.

## 4:00-5:30 - Introduce The Legend Card Collection

On screen, show: `Promote/screenshots/02-legends-collection-top-desktop.png`.

This is where the app becomes different.

The Legend Card Collection is the heart of the experience. Think about it like a football album, but connected to real stories and YouTube videos.

Every card has a reason to exist. We do not want duplicate cards. We do not want random cards. We want each card to connect to one story, one moment, one player, one nation, one episode, or one special reason.

When users open the collection, they can see cards that are locked, cards that are ready, cards they collected, and cards connected to YouTube episodes.

The goal is simple:

Read the preview. Listen to the story. Watch the YouTube episode. Come back and collect the card.

That is the loop.

This is the product structure we want people to understand. YouTube gives the story. The app gives the collection. The card gives the reason to come back.

## 5:30-7:00 - Explain The Card Journey

On screen, show: `Promote/screenshots/03-collector-quest.png`.

Here is the collection journey.

First, the user reads the card preview. That gives them the hook. It tells them why this card matters.

Second, the user can listen to the story inside the app. The voice experience is important because not everyone wants to read everything. Some people want to hear the story while browsing.

Third, the user opens the matching YouTube video. This is where the full story lives. The YouTube channel is not separate from the app. It is part of the same machine.

Fourth, the user returns and collects the card.

That is the behavior we want to build:

Open app. Discover story. Watch video. Unlock card. Return tomorrow for the next one.

For a football fan, this becomes very natural. Every match has context. Every nation has history. Every player has a story. The app turns all of that into collectible cards.

## 7:00-8:30 - Did You Know Cards

On screen, show: `Promote/screenshots/04-did-you-know-cards-grid.png` and `Promote/cards/sheets/did-you-know-card-sheet.png`.

The first special category is Did You Know cards.

These are short football history stories. They are perfect for Shorts because they are fast, emotional, and surprising.

A viewer watches a short story and then comes into the app to unlock the matching card.

For example, a card can be about a forgotten World Cup hero, a strange football fact, a player who carried a nation, or a moment most people never heard about.

This is very powerful because football fans love hidden stories. They love saying, "I did not know that." And once they learn the story, the card feels more valuable.

The card is not just an image. It becomes a memory of the story.

That is why Did You Know cards are one of the best ways to bring new users from YouTube into the app.

## 8:30-10:00 - Episode Cards

On screen, show: `Promote/screenshots/05-episode-series-cards-grid.png` and the episode sheets in `Promote/cards/sheets/`.

The second category is episode cards.

These connect to the main WorldCup26 Legends series. Each episode can be built around a match, a rivalry, a nation, a player battle, or a story angle.

The key rule is that each episode deserves its own card. If the YouTube video is about Croatia versus Ghana, that story should unlock its own card. If the video is about Belgium versus New Zealand, that needs a different card.

No duplicates. No lazy reuse.

This matters because collectors notice. If users feel every card is unique, they trust the collection. They feel the album has value.

Episode cards are also great for live content because we can say:

"Watch the episode, then collect the card."

That gives every YouTube upload a second life inside the app.

## 10:00-11:15 - Supporter Wallpaper Cards

On screen, show: `Promote/screenshots/06-supporter-wallpaper-cards-grid.png` and `Promote/cards/sheets/supporter-card-sheet.png`.

The third category is supporter wallpaper cards.

These are portrait cards built for phones. They are made for fans who want something beautiful, downloadable, and connected to their nation.

There are 48 nations, so each nation can have its own supporter card.

This is important for sharing. A Brazil fan wants Brazil. A Mexico fan wants Mexico. A Japan fan wants Japan. A Ghana fan wants Ghana.

That turns the app into something personal.

People do not only collect random football cards. They collect identity. They collect the nation they support. They collect the story they believe in.

And because these cards are portrait format, they are strong for mobile, stories, wallpapers, and social posts.

## 11:15-12:15 - Bonus Legend Cards

On screen, show: `Promote/screenshots/07-bonus-cards-grid.png` and `Promote/cards/sheets/bonus-card-sheet.png`.

The fourth category is bonus cards.

Bonus cards are special. They should not be too many. They should feel rare.

Right now the important bonus cards are Lukaku, Luis Diaz, and World Cup Monopoly.

These are not normal episode cards. They are special moments, special player stories, or special channel events.

That gives us another layer in the collection:

There are regular episode cards, Did You Know cards, supporter cards, and then rare bonus cards.

For collectors, this matters. They want categories. They want rarity. They want a reason to check what is new.

## 12:15-13:30 - Why YouTube And The App Work Together

On screen, switch between the YouTube channel and the app collection.

The YouTube channel and the app are not two separate projects.

The app captures that emotion and turns it into a collectible action.

If someone watches a story on YouTube and then leaves, the relationship can disappear. But if they watch the story and unlock a card, now they have a reason to return.

That is the structure:

YouTube brings attention.
The app creates collection.
The cards create return behavior.
The leaderboard creates competition.
The next video creates the next unlock.

That is why this can keep growing.

## 15:00-17:00 - The App Structure In One Simple Map

On screen, show the Legend Card Collection again, then scroll slowly through the tabs.

Let me explain the structure one more time in a very simple way, because this is the part that makes the app different.

There are three engines working together.

The first engine is the game engine. That is where you sign up, pick 3 teams, follow matches, and see the leaderboard. This gives people a reason to check the app during the tournament.

The second engine is the story engine. That is the YouTube channel. Every episode, every short, every bonus video gives us a story that people can watch and feel.

The third engine is the collection engine. That is the album inside the app. This is where those stories become cards.

So the user does not need to think about technology. They just feel the loop:

I picked my teams.
I found a story.
I watched the video.
I unlocked the card.
I want the next card.

That is what we are building.

And this is why the app can keep growing. We can add more matches, more stories, more cards, more nations, more competitions, and more reasons to come back.

The important thing is that everything stays easy. If the app becomes confusing, people leave. So the structure is built around clear tabs: Legends, Series, Wallpapers, Bonus, matches, leaderboard, and account.

The collection should feel like an album you understand immediately.
