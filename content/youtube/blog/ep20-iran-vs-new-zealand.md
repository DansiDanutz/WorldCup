## The secret nobody talks about

South Africa, 2010. New Zealand — ranked near the bottom of the whole tournament. Game one, against Slovakia: ninety-third minute, losing, and Winston Reid rises to head it in. 1-1. Their first ever World Cup point. Game two: the defending world champions, Italy. New Zealand score first and hold on. 1-1. The tiny nation refuses to fall. Game three, Paraguay: 0-0. Three games, three draws, and not a single defeat. They went home unbeaten — the only team at the entire World Cup that never lost. And Italy, the champions? Out, bottom of the group.

## Team Melli and the door they cannot open

Across the pitch tonight, Iran. Team Melli, with their own piece of World Cup folklore — France, 1998, where in a match heavy with politics Iran beat the United States 2-1 for their first ever World Cup win. Yet here is the ache they share with New Zealand: in all their history, Iran have never once escaped the group. Two nations chasing the same door. Iran are led by Mehdi Taremi, the man they call the Iranian Ronaldo, who once worked a gas station before football gave him a second life. Beside him, captain Alireza Jahanbakhsh, who went to Europe and came back a leader, with Saman Ghoddos pulling the strings, Saeid Ezatolahi shielding the back and Mehdi Ghaedi running at tired legs. Disciplined, organised, hungry.

## The All Whites and their giant

New Zealand — the All Whites — and up top, a giant. Chris Wood, the Auckland boy who became his country's greatest ever scorer. Around him, Marko Stamenic driving midfield, Liberato Cacace flying down the left, with Barbarouses and Sarpreet Singh chasing more history. So here is the duel: Iran's discipline against New Zealand's heart, Taremi's craft against Wood's power.
