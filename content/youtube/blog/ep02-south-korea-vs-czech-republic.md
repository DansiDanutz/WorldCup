## The secret nobody talks about
In nearly a century of football, these two nations have NEVER met at a World Cup. Not once. Four meetings, all friendlies, and every one left a mark. A wild 2-2 draw in Seoul in 1998. Then Prague, 2001: Pavel Nedved and the Czechs crushed Korea 5-0. The humiliation cut so deep that Korean newspapers gave their brand-new coach a cruel nickname — Mister Five-Zero. That coach was Guus Hiddink. One year later, those same wounded warriors stunned Italy, shocked Spain, and danced into the semi-finals of their own World Cup. The 5-0 didn't break Korea. It built the miracle. And in 2016, in Prague, Korea finally took its revenge, 2-1. The scar was settled — but the World Cup chapter was never written, until tonight.

## The Taegeuk Warriors and the Czech bloodline
Meet tonight's South Korea: nine straight World Cups, five warriors carrying the flame. Son Heung-min, 33, in his fourth and final World Cup — every touch now a goodbye written in lightning. Behind him stands The Monster, Kim Min-jae, the wall that conquered Italy with Napoli; strikers see him in their nightmares. And pulling the strings, Lee Kang-in, the magician from Paris who turns defenders into spectators.

But the Czechs are heirs to a dynasty — two World Cup finals, a European title, and the Panenka, the most audacious penalty football has ever seen. Patrik Schick, the marksman who once scored from the halfway line at a European Championship, lives in the margins. Tomas Soucek is the two-metre tower of Prague. Adam Hlozek is the young wolf hungry to drag Czech football back into the light.

Every great match hides a secret duel: The Monster against the Marksman. Kim Min-jae against Patrik Schick. Ninety minutes, one cage, one escape artist. One of them will own this night; the other will dream about it forever.
