## The secret nobody talks about

In 1974, Haiti became the first Caribbean nation ever to reach a World Cup, and they walked out against mighty Italy. Italy's goalkeeper was Dino Zoff, a man who had not conceded a single goal in 1,142 minutes — the longest clean-sheet streak in football history. Then, in the 46th minute, Emmanuel Sanon ran onto a pass, rounded Zoff, and scored. The wall fell. Haiti lost the match, but Sanon's goal became a flag forever: proof that the small can wound the giant. Fifty-two years later, through earthquakes and through everything, Haiti is back.

## The kings arrive

Brazil are the only nation to have played in every World Cup ever held — five titles to their name. For them, anything less than victory is treated as a national emergency. Vinicius Junior, 25, is the dancer whose feet move faster than the music. Neymar, 34, plays his very last World Cup with the freedom of a man who has nothing left to prove. Raphinha tortures the flanks, and Alisson stands behind them all as the calmest man alive. On paper it is a mismatch — samba royalty against a squad few outside the Caribbean could name.

## The nation that fears nothing

But Haiti did not cross the sea to bow. Their striker, Duckens Nazon, was found by a scout while he was stacking shelves in a supermarket. Now he leads a World Cup attack, wearing that story like armor. Derrick Etienne Junior was born in America and chose Haiti. Johny Placide is the wall of Port-au-Prince. Every one of them knows Sanon's story by heart, and a nation that survives everything fears nothing. The duel inside the match is Neymar against Nazon — the man who has everything against the man who came from nothing, both playing for pure joy.
