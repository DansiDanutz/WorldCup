## The secret nobody talks about

Moscow, the Luzhniki Stadium, the 11th of July, 2018 — a World Cup semi-final. After five minutes, Kieran Trippier bent in a free kick. England, one to nothing, and around the world four words sang out: it's coming home. But Croatia would not bow. On sixty-eight minutes, Ivan Perisic hooked it level, and the giant started to wobble. Extra time, the hundred-and-ninth minute: Mario Mandzukic. A nation of four million had broken England's heart and reached their first World Cup final. Their captain, Luka Modric, was named the best player of the whole tournament. And tonight, eight years on, comes the rematch.

## The Three Lions, grown up

But this England has grown up. The Three Lions are world champions exactly once, in 1966, and aching ever since for one more. Harry Kane, the captain and England's all-time leading scorer, chases the one prize that has always escaped him. Around him, Jude Bellingham, Bukayo Saka and Phil Foden — a midfield jewel and two wingers in their prime, an attack that can hurt anyone. The deepest squad on the planet.

## Croatia, the tiny giants

But across the line stands the team that has done the impossible before. Croatia, the Vatreni, are the greatest overachievers football has ever seen — three World Cup podiums from a country of under four million. And still, at the heart of them, Luka Modric, the maestro, the Golden Ball, the man who simply will not stop running. With Josko Gvardiol and Mateo Kovacic, steel front to back, and Andrej Kramaric ice-cold in front of goal. So here is the duel: England's depth against Croatia's scars, the giant who has everything to prove against the ghost who's done it all before.
