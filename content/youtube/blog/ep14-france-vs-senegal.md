## The secret nobody talks about

These two nations have met only once where it truly mattered — the opening match of the 2002 World Cup. France arrived reigning world champions, reigning European champions, untouchable. Senegal arrived a former French colony at their very first World Cup, with a squad raised in the French leagues. Nobody gave them a prayer. In the thirtieth minute, Papa Bouba Diop bundled the ball over the line, and the unthinkable was real: Senegal one, France nil. The colony had humbled the empire. France went home without scoring a single goal; Senegal marched to the quarter-finals. That night became a symbol of African football, and of defiance.

## The empire reborn: France's attacking storm

Twenty-four years later, France are champions again, and three names carry their storm. Kylian Mbappé — the Prince of Bondy, Real Madrid's number ten, thirty-eight kilometres an hour of pure intent, a man who scored a hat-trick in a World Cup final and still lost. Beside him, Ousmane Dembélé — two feet, no weakness, a dribble that ties defenders in knots and a Ballon d'Or in his hands. And anchoring it all, Aurélien Tchouaméni, the metronome who decides the tempo before the game even begins.

## The lion still hunts: the kings of Africa

But Senegal are no underdogs now — they are champions of Africa, the Lions of Teranga. Sadio Mané, the King of Teranga and the Dakar Express, is his nation's greatest ever scorer: a man who once walked four hundred kilometres for a dream, then built his village a hospital, a school and a mosque with the reward. Nicolas Jackson is the blade on the counter, all sprint and ice, waiting for one Mbappé mistake. And Kalidou Koulibaly is the captain and the wall, the calm that turns panic into order. The duel inside the match is the Prince against the King — Mbappé's lightning against Mané's relentless, ageless will. Speed versus soul.

## The night the lion roared in Los Angeles

In our story, France strike first. In the fifty-sixth minute Mbappé receives on the left and explodes, cuts inside, and buries it — the Prince silences seventy thousand souls and the empire stands tall again. But watch the man in green refuse to drop his head. Mbappé runs again, and this time, at thirty-four years old, Mané tracks every stride, slides, and wins the ball clean. He springs up, drives, slips it to Jackson on the break — and the equaliser flies in. The lion roars in Los Angeles.
