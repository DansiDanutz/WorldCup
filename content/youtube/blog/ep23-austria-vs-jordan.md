## The secret nobody talks about

Austria once had a team they simply called the Wunderteam — the wonder team. In the 1930s they tore Europe apart: six-nil over Germany, eight-one over Switzerland, football like nobody had seen. Their heartbeat was Matthias Sindelar, so graceful they called him the Mozart of Football. They reached a World Cup semifinal in 1934 — and then they fell. Austria have never, not once, won a World Cup. Nearly a century of waiting for the wonder team's heirs is the weight Austria carry tonight. And across from them, Jordan's own fairytale: at the 2023 Asian Cup they faced South Korea, ranked sixty places above them, and won 2-0 — Al-Naimat, then Al-Tamari. A nation that had never reached a final was suddenly in one. They lost it to Qatar, but the world had seen them now. Then, after forty years and nine failed campaigns, Jordan qualified for their first-ever World Cup.

## Old royalty: the heirs of the Wunderteam

Now, Austria. Led by David Alaba — a defender who has won everything in the club game, captaining the heirs of the Wunderteam. Marcel Sabitzer drives the midfield, Konrad Laimer never stops running, and Christoph Baumgartner arrives late in the box. And up front, Marko Arnautović, the old fox, still dangerous, still smiling. This is Austrian steel and pedigree, carrying a century of hurt that demands an answer.

## The Brave Ones with nothing to lose

But Jordan, the Nashama — the Brave Ones — have weapons too. Musa Al-Tamari, quick, fearless, the man who lit up that Asian Cup. And Yazan Al-Naimat, the striker who scores on the nights that matter most. So here is the duel: Austrian experience and pedigree against the fearless joy of a nation with nothing to lose. Until now Jordan were only ever a nearly-team — but they did not come this far to be quiet on their debut.
