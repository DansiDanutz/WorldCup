## The secret nobody talks about

And this one is true. Rosario, two thousand five hundred miles from home, the second of June 1978. Tunisia, written off before kickoff, beat Mexico three-one — and became the first African nation ever to win a match at a World Cup. No African team had ever done it. After that day, the whole continent believed. The door was open. And they kept knocking: in 2022, in Qatar, Tunisia beat reigning world champions France one to nothing. Yet here is the ache — across all those tournaments, Tunisia have never once escaped the group. The door they opened, they have never walked through.

Sweden are forged in long winters and hard pitches, and right now they are blessed with two of the most feared strikers on Earth. Viktor Gyökeres is the freight train. A few years ago he couldn't hold a place in the Premier League — written off, shipped out. He went away, scored a mountain of goals, and came back a beast: all muscle, all menace, the man defenders now have nightmares about. And beside him, Alexander Isak. Where Gyökeres is power, Isak is grace — so smooth defenders forget to move. Power and elegance, side by side.

And in midfield, Hannibal Mejbri, once told he wasn't good enough for the Premier League, now plays every minute like he's still proving them wrong.
