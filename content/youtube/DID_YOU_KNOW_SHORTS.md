# "Did You Know?" — WorldCup Legends Shorts Bank

Hook-first Shorts (30–40s) built on **real, verified, little-known** World Cup player
stories. Channel rules apply: Brian VO carries everything, **no on-screen sentence
text** (labels only), **100% AI Pixar visuals · soccer-only · no real footage/logos**,
made-for-kids = NO, cleared music. Every story is fact-checked with a source — never
invent (hard rule #9). Each Short opens with a `DID YOU KNOW?` title card (≤4 words, allowed).

Structure each Short: **Hook (0–4s, curiosity gap) → build → payoff/twist → tag (subscribe)**.

## FORMAT — PORTRAIT 9:16 (1080×1920), mandatory
All Shorts are **vertical**. Production spec:
- Generate every image and image→video clip at **aspect_ratio `9:16`** (1080×1920), not 16:9.
- **Frame for vertical:** subject centered, head-and-shoulders / full-body upright comps; action reads top-to-bottom. Avoid wide horizontal compositions (they crop badly).
- **Mobile safe zones:** keep the `DID YOU KNOW?` card and the player-name label in the **middle 80% vertically** — leave ~12% top and ~18% bottom clear of the YouTube UI (timestamp, title, like/subscribe, caption).
- **Length:** 30–40s. ~5 beats; ease 5s clips to fit (Seedance→Kling fallback, same as the Messi build).
- Big bold short labels only (player name, year). `DID YOU KNOW?` opener card upper-third.

### DC-1 — The One-Armed Champion (Héctor Castro) ⭐ recommended next
**Hook:** A man with one arm scored in the **first-ever World Cup final** — and won it.
**Arc:** At 13, Héctor Castro lost his right forearm in an electric-saw accident. Uruguay called him
"El Divino Manco" — "the divine one-armed one." He scored Uruguay's first-ever World Cup goal, then the
**final, decisive 4th goal** in the 1930 final (Uruguay 4–2 Argentina) — the world's first champions.
**Why obscure:** almost nobody knows the first World Cup was won with a one-armed centre-forward scoring in the final.
**Sources:** [Wikipedia — Héctor Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_Castro) · A Halftime Report · qatarmoments.
*Confidence: rock-solid (amputation as a teen; scored in the 1930 final; Uruguay champions).*

### DC-2 — The Hat-Trick That Didn't Exist For 76 Years (Bert Patenaude)
**Hook:** He scored the **first hat-trick in World Cup history** — and the record books ignored it for 76 years.
**Arc:** USA's Bert Patenaude scored 3 vs Paraguay on 17 July 1930. For decades FIFA credited his 2nd goal to
a teammate/own goal, so the milestone "belonged to no one." Only on **10 Nov 2006** — after a historian's review —
did FIFA confirm Patenaude as the first. He'd died in 1974, never knowing he'd be credited.
**Why obscure:** the first WC hat-trick is a quiz "fact" with a forgotten, bittersweet backstory.
**Sources:** [Wikipedia — Bert Patenaude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Patenaude) · US Soccer · Yahoo Sports.
*Confidence: rock-solid (FIFA confirmed 2006; 76-year gap).*

### DC-3 — The Most Misunderstood Man in World Cup History (Mwepu Ilunga, Zaire 1974)
**Hook:** The world laughed at him for 40 years for one "stupid" moment — the truth is heartbreaking.
**Arc:** Vs Brazil, 1974, Zaire's Mwepu Ilunga charged out of the wall and booted a free-kick away before it
was taken — replayed forever as football's dumbest blunder. The truth: the players learned their promised bonuses
were **stolen** by Mobutu-regime cronies; threatened, unpaid and humiliated, Ilunga deliberately tried to get
**sent off** in protest. Many of that team later **died in poverty**. A "joke" that was really a cry of defiance.
**Why obscure:** everyone knows the clip; almost no one knows what it really meant.
**Sources:** [Wikipedia — Mwepu Ilunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwepu_Ilunga) · ESPN (his own words) · The Greatness Index.
*Confidence: rock-solid on the incident + bonus dispute (his own L'Équipe/ESPN account).* **Tone:** dignity restored, not mockery.

### DC-4 — The Secret Heroes (Pak Doo-ik & North Korea, 1966)
**Hook:** An unknown factory worker knocked mighty Italy out of the World Cup — then vanished behind the Iron Curtain.
**Arc:** 1966, Middlesbrough. North Korea, 1000-1 outsiders, faced Italy. A Pyongyang **print-factory labourer**,
Pak Doo-ik, scored — Korea 1, Italy 0 — and Korea reached the quarter-finals (first Asian team to). The team went
home behind a closed border; the adoring town of Middlesbrough didn't see the survivors again until **2002**.
**Why obscure:** the upset is known; the men — and the eerie aftermath — are not.
**Sources:** [FIFA — Korea DPR v Italy 1966](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/korea-dpr-italy-1966) ·
[Wikipedia — Pak Doo-ik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pak_Doo-ik) · Sky HISTORY.
*Confidence: rock-solid on goal/upset/QF + the 2002 Middlesbrough return; the alleged "punishment" is via defector accounts — frame as "it's said," never asserted.*

### DC-5 — Four Goals, And Erased From History (Ernst Wilimowski, 1938)
**Hook:** He scored **4 goals in one World Cup match** — a first that stood for decades — and was knocked out the same day.
**Arc:** 1938, Poland vs Brazil. Wilimowski became the first man to score **four** in a WC match and dragged Poland
level — yet Brazil won 6–5 in extra time and Poland went home. A Silesian caught between nations, after the Nazi
invasion he played for **Germany**, and Poland erased him as a traitor. A staggering display by a man both
countries tried to forget.
**Why obscure:** the 4-goal feat is a quiz fact; the man's erasure is a forgotten tragedy.
**Sources:** [Wikipedia — Ernst Wilimowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Wilimowski) · Notes From Poland · ESPN.
*Confidence: rock-solid on the 4 goals / 6–5 / later Germany caps.* **Tone:** war context sober & factual; no swastika imagery; soccer-only.

### DC-6 — The Greatest Footballer Who Never Played (Carlos "Kaiser" Henrique Raposo) ⭐ owner-requested
**Hook:** For almost 20 years Brazil's biggest clubs signed him… and he never played one real match — *on purpose.*
**Arc (DRAMA → IDOL, played as the greatest con in football):** Carlos Henrique Raposo — "Kaiser" — talked his
way onto Botafogo, Flamengo, Fluminense, Vasco, Bangu and clubs abroad without ever truly playing. He "pulled"
hamstrings warming up, faked fitness, and leaned on real stars who vouched for him (Carlos Alberto Torres,
Renato Gaúcho, Bebeto). The legend moment: handed a debut at Bangu by owner Castor de Andrade, Kaiser climbed
into the crowd and started a fight with fans to get himself sent off *before kicking a ball* — then told the
owner he was defending his honour against insults, and got his contract **extended.** Final stats: **0 goals,
0 games.** The payoff/idol twist: he became immortal *precisely for never playing* — "the greatest footballer
never to play football."
**Why it WOWs:** a real man fooled professional football for two decades and is now a celebrated folk legend.
**Caveat (read before scripting):** this is football **FOLKLORE, not a World Cup match story** — Kaiser never
played, so frame it as a standalone "Did You Know?" legend, **not** WC canon. Several gags (the Bangu brawl,
bribed physios, fake foreign phone calls) are widely reported but best framed **"it's said / famously,"** and
keep the gangster-owner (Castor de Andrade) context light — **no crime glamorisation** (monetization rules #0/#8),
playful/admiring tone, soccer-only, no real footage/logos.
**Sources:** [Wikipedia — Carlos Kaiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Kaiser_(footballer)) · documentary
*Kaiser: The Greatest Footballer Never to Play Football* (Tribeca world premiere 21 Apr 2018; features Carlos
Alberto Torres, Zico, Renato Gaúcho, Bebeto, Júnior, Ricardo Rocha) · book *The Greatest Footballer Never to Play
Football* (Rob Smyth). *Confidence: rock-solid that he was signed by pro clubs yet effectively never played (0 apps)
and on the documentary; specific gags framed "it's said."*
