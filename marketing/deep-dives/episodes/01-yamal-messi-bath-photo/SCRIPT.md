# Messi Bathed Lamine Yamal as a Baby — 19 Years Later They Met in the World Cup Final | Did You Know?

Narrator: **Brian** (ElevenLabs). 697 words, about 5 minutes.

## Cold open

New Jersey. The nineteenth of July, twenty twenty-six. The World Cup final. Spain against Argentina. And after ninety minutes, nobody has scored.

On one side, Lionel Messi. Thirty-nine years old, chasing one last crown.

On the other, Lamine Yamal. Nineteen. The boy the whole world calls the next Messi.

But here is the secret almost nobody in that stadium knew. These two had met before. Nineteen years earlier. And Messi was holding him, in a bathtub.

## The raffle

Summer, two thousand and seven. Barcelona's club foundation, UNICEF and the newspaper Diario Sport are making a charity calendar. Families from the neighbourhoods around Barcelona will be photographed at the Camp Nou, with a first-team player.

Up the coast in Mataró, in a working-class district called Rocafonda, families sign up for a raffle. One young couple wins it.

The father, Mounir, is from Morocco. The mother, Sheila, from Equatorial Guinea. Their son is only weeks old. His name is Lamine.

And the player who will hold him is Barcelona's shy twenty-year-old from Rosario. That spring, against Getafe, he had dribbled past half a team, in a goal so like Maradona's that all of Spain was talking about it.

Lionel Messi.

## The photo

The photographer, Joan Monfort, fills a small plastic tub with water. Messi is asked to bathe the baby for the camera. He is nervous. Careful. Gentle. Click.

The calendar is printed. It raises its money. And then the photos simply vanish. For seventeen years, almost nobody sees them.

In those seventeen years, Messi becomes the greatest player of his generation. Eight Ballon d'Or awards. And in Qatar, in twenty twenty-two, the World Cup he had chased all his life.

And every time he scores, he points to the sky. For his grandmother Celia, the woman who first walked him to football, in Rosario.

## The boy from three-zero-four

Meanwhile, the baby from the bathtub grows up in Rocafonda, the ball always at his feet. At six, Barcelona bring him into La Masia. The same academy that made Messi.

April twenty twenty-three. At fifteen years and two hundred and ninety days old, he makes his first-team debut.

And when Yamal scores, he makes a sign with his fingers. Three. Zero. Four. The last digits of Rocafonda's postcode. Like Messi pointing to the sky, he points back to where it all began.

Summer, twenty twenty-four. The European Championship semi-final, against France. Yamal is sixteen. He cuts inside from the right and curls the ball into the top corner.

The youngest scorer in the history of the Euros. Four days later, he turns seventeen. Five days later, Spain are champions of Europe.

That summer, his father, Mounir, digs out the old photos and posts one online. Messi, and a baby in a tub. His caption: the beginning of two legends.

The world could hardly believe it. Messi had bathed the boy now being called the next Messi.

## The final

On the road to New Jersey, against Saudi Arabia, Yamal scores his first World Cup goal. Only Gavi had ever scored for Spain at a World Cup at a younger age.

And so, nineteen years after the bathtub, Messi and Yamal walk out together, for the biggest match in football.

Emiliano Martínez saves from Yamal. At the other end, Unai Simón denies Messi.

Ninety minutes. Nothing. Extra time.

Then, in the hundred and sixth minute, Ferran Torres finishes from close range. One-nil, Spain.

Spain hold on. They are champions of the world.

Messi takes the Silver Ball, the second-best player of the tournament. And Lamine Yamal, the baby he once held in a plastic tub, is a world champion at nineteen.

A charity raffle in Rocafonda. A shy twenty-year-old, a tub of water and a baby. Nineteen years later, the same two names, Messi and Yamal, in the same World Cup final. One points to the sky. The other, to three-zero-four.
